Sine, Cosine, and Tangent

Trigonometry is a branch of mathematics that studies the relationships between the angles and sides of triangles. Three of the most important trigonometric functions are sine (sin), cosine (cos), and tangent (tan).

What are sin, cos, and tan?

In a right-angled triangle, there are three main sides:

Hypotenuse – the longest side, always opposite the 90° angle.

Opposite side – the side opposite the angle being analyzed.

Adjacent side – the side next to that angle (other than the hypotenuse).

The three trigonometric ratios are calculated as follows:
sin(angle) = opposite side ÷ hypotenuse
cos(angle) = adjacent side ÷ hypotenuse
tan(angle) = opposite side ÷ adjacent side
An easy way to memorize this is using the acronym SOH-CAH-TOA:

SOH → Sine = Opposite / Hypotenuse

CAH → Cosine = Adjacent / Hypotenuse

TOA → Tangent = Opposite / Adjacent

Angle table

Some angles are particularly important in trigonometry. These include 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, and 90°.
Angle | sin | cos | tan
0° | 0 | 1 | 0
30° | 1/2 | √3/2 | √3/3
45° | √2/2 | √2/2 | 1
60° | √3/2 | 1/2 | √3
90° | 1 | 0 | undefined

Why is the 45° angle important?

In a right-angled triangle where one of the other angles is 45°, the two legs (catheti) have the same length.  Therefore, the sine and cosine of 45° have the same value:
sin(45°) = cos(45°) = √2/2
Since the tangent is the opposite side divided by the adjacent side, and both have the same length:
tan(45°) = 1

How did trigonometry originate?

Trigonometry was not invented by a single person. It was developed over many centuries by different peoples and mathematicians.
The earliest knowledge related to trigonometry emerged in antiquity, primarily due to the need to study astronomy, measure distances, and understand the movements of celestial bodies.
The Babylonians already utilized mathematical knowledge related to angles and astronomy. Later, the Greeks—especially mathematicians and astronomers like Hipparchus—developed methods to relate angles to lengths.
The word "trigonometry" comes from Greek and roughly means "measurement of triangles."
During the Middle Ages, mathematicians from the Islamic world contributed significantly to the development of trigonometric functions and their tables. Centuries later, European mathematicians continued this work, creating increasingly precise methods for calculating sine, cosine, and tangent.

What is trigonometry used for?

Today, trigonometry is used in many fields, such as:

Architecture and engineering, to calculate heights, distances, and slopes;

Astronomy, to study the positions and movements of celestial bodies;

Physics, to study forces and motion;

Geography, to calculate distances and positions;

Computing and graphics, to create movement and represent shapes;

Navigation, to determine directions and locations.

Thus, although it began thousands of years ago with problems related primarily to measurement and astronomy, trigonometry remains a vital tool in mathematics and science.

Conclusion

Sine, cosine, and tangent are fundamental concepts in trigonometry.  Through them, we can relate the angles to the sides of a triangle and solve many mathematical and scientific problems.
The table of notable angles—specifically the values ​​of 30°, 45°, 60°, and 90°—allows for quick calculations. The 45° angle stands out because the sine and cosine have exactly the same value, and the tangent is equal to 1.
Trigonometry is, therefore, an example of how mathematical knowledge developed centuries ago continues to be used in the modern world.
